# Escalation ladders (India)

**Last verified: 29 October 2026** (banking section re-checked against RBI's RB-IOS 2026 FAQ and the 2019 TAT circular; railway TDR windows not yet confirmed on an official page). Timelines, limits and portals change. Confirm on the official portal before giving the user a date, especially for banking (a new RBI scheme took effect on 1 July 2026) and telecom (TRAI was consulting on amendments to its complaint regulations in 2026).

Every figure in this file is a starting point for verification, not a final answer. Under the skill's verification gate, any figure not re-checked against an official source in the current session must be shown to the user as UNVERIFIED.

General rule for every sector: **the first step is always a written complaint to the company itself**, with a reference number saved. Regulators and ombudsmen will ask for it.

## Route types

Each route below is tagged with its type. Routes are not always consecutive rungs; check the tags before recommending an order.

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **[PREREQ]** | Must be done first; later forums check for proof of it | Written complaint to the company; insurer's GRO; operator's complaint centre |
| **[PARALLEL]** | Optional; can run alongside other routes without harming them | National Consumer Helpline, CPGRAMS, RTI, AirSewa |
| **[FORUM]** | Formal body that decides the dispute and issues an order or award | RBI Ombudsman, Insurance Ombudsman, Consumer Commission |
| **[EXCLUSIVE]** | Choosing it can close or bar another forum | Filing in a consumer commission or court bars the RBI and Insurance Ombudsman for the same matter; an ombudsman complaint already decided on merits cannot be refiled there |

**Consumer commissions have no legal prerequisite.** The Consumer Protection Act doesn't require a prior complaint to the company. Where a company step is tagged [PREREQ] below, it is a prerequisite for the *ombudsman, appellate authority or regulator*; for the commission it is **recommended evidence**, so never tell a user they *cannot* go to the commission without it.

Recommended default order where both exist: company **[PREREQ]** → the sector ombudsman **[FORUM]** (free, faster, capped) → consumer commission **[FORUM]** only if the ombudsman route is unavailable, rejected, or the claim exceeds its cap. Always confirm the current exclusion clauses for the specific scheme.

## 1. Banks, NBFCs, credit cards, UPI apps and wallets (RBI-regulated)

**Rung 1 [PREREQ]: The bank or NBFC.** Written complaint through the official grievance portal, email or branch. Save the complaint number.
**Rung 2 [PREREQ, recommended]: Principal Nodal Officer / Grievance Redressal Officer.** Contact details must be displayed on the bank's website and branches. Escalate here if customer care stalls.
**Rung 3 [FORUM, EXCLUSIVE]: RBI Ombudsman under the Reserve Bank – Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (RB-IOS), 2026.**
- Effective 1 July 2026; replaced the 2021 scheme. Per RBI's FAQ, only complaints **received by the Ombudsman** before 1 July 2026 stay under the 2021 scheme. A complaint filed today falls under the 2026 scheme even if the bank's reply came earlier.
- **Transition risk:** the FAQ is silent on cases where the bank's reply came before 1 July 2026. Under the 2021 scheme the user had one year; under 2026 it is 90 days. Tell the user both readings exist, treat the shorter one as binding for planning, and point them to the consumer commission (two-year limit) as the fallback.
- Eligible when the bank rejects the complaint fully or partly, the user is dissatisfied with the reply, or there's no reply within 30 days of the bank receiving the complaint.
- **User deadline: within 90 days** of whichever is **later**: the expiry of the 30-day reply period, or the bank's last communication. (One year under the old scheme; users often assume they still have that long.) The original complaint to the bank must itself be within the Limitation Act period.
- **Covered entities** (RBI FAQ on RB-IOS 2026, Q13): all commercial banks, regional rural banks, state and central cooperative banks; scheduled urban cooperative banks; non-scheduled urban cooperative banks with deposits of ₹50 crore or more; RBI-registered NBFCs, excluding housing finance companies, core investment companies and certain specialised types, with deposit-taking or customer-facing NBFCs subject to the asset-size threshold in the FAQ; non-bank prepaid payment instrument issuers (wallets); credit information companies. **Not covered:** cooperative credit societies, housing finance companies (complain to NHB), chit funds and unregistered lenders. Check the FAQ's Q13 and Q14 before routing any small NBFC or cooperative.
- Dispute amount: no limit on the amount in dispute.
- File free at **cms.rbi.org.in**, by email to crpc@rbi.org.in, or by post to the Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre (CRPC). Helpline: **14448**.
- Compensation caps: up to ₹30 lakh for consequential loss, plus up to ₹3 lakh for harassment, time, expenses and mental anguish.
- Appeal against the Ombudsman's decision to the Appellate Authority within 30 days.
- Not maintainable if the same matter is already before a court, consumer commission or arbitrator.

**Failed digital transactions: automatic compensation (check this first).** RBI's circular "Harmonisation of Turn Around Time (TAT) and customer compensation for failed transactions using authorised Payment Systems" (RBI/2019-20/67, 20 September 2019) sets outer limits for reversing failed transactions, with **₹100 per day** compensation credited automatically beyond them, no claim needed. Key rows (T = transaction day):
- UPI fund transfer, debited but beneficiary not credited: reversal by T+1, then ₹100/day.
- UPI merchant payment, debited but merchant not confirmed: T+5, then ₹100/day.
- IMPS, debited but beneficiary not credited: T+1, then ₹100/day.
- ATM / micro-ATM, debited but cash not dispensed: T+5, then ₹100/day.
- Card-to-card transfer: T+1. Card at PoS, no charge slip: T+5. Both ₹100/day after.
- NACH auto-debit, delayed credit or reversal: T+1, then ₹100/day.
For any failed UPI, IMPS, ATM, card or NACH transaction, compute the TAT, add the compensation owed to the relief sought, and cite the circular in the letter. A double debit or wrong debit that isn't a "failed transaction" under the circular's table goes through the normal ladder. Source: rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11693.

**Fraud and unauthorised transactions (urgent path):**
- Block the card/account immediately via app or helpline.
- Call **1930** (national cyber fraud helpline) and report at **cybercrime.gov.in**.
- Report to the bank in writing at once. Under RBI's customer-liability rules, reporting within 3 working days generally limits the customer's liability for unauthorised electronic transactions where the customer wasn't negligent. Later reporting shifts more liability onto the customer.
- Then follow the normal ladder above for the refund.

## 2. Insurance (IRDAI-regulated: life, health, motor, general)

**Rung 1 [PREREQ]: Insurer's Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO).** Insurers must acknowledge immediately and resolve within **14 days**.
**Rung 2 [PARALLEL, recommended]: IRDAI via Bima Bharosa** (bimabharosa.irdai.gov.in), helplines **155255** / **1800 4254 732**, or complaints@irdai.gov.in. Bima Bharosa is linked to insurers' complaint systems but does **not** auto-escalate to the Ombudsman.
**Rung 3 [FORUM, EXCLUSIVE]: Insurance Ombudsman (Bima Lokpal)**, under the Insurance Ombudsman Rules, 2017.
- Eligible when the insurer rejects the complaint, the reply is unsatisfactory, or there's no reply within 30 days.
- **User deadline: within one year** of the insurer's final reply, or one year after the 30-day period if no reply.
- Claim value limit: up to ₹50 lakh.
- File online, by email, or at the Ombudsman office with jurisdiction over the insurer's branch.
- Free. Award binds the insurer, not the policyholder.
**Alternative [FORUM]: Consumer commission** (see section 7). Filing here bars the Ombudsman for the same matter.

## 3. Telecom (mobile, broadband, DTH via operator)

TRAI is a regulator and does not resolve individual complaints.

**Rung 1 [PREREQ]: Operator's complaint centre.** Get a docket number. Service faults and billing issues have short resolution windows under TRAI quality-of-service rules.
**Rung 2 [PREREQ for appeal]: Operator's Appellate Authority**, one per telecom circle. Details must be published on the operator's website (for example, Airtel's per-circle appellate page). Appeal in writing with the docket number if unresolved or unsatisfactory. Check the appeal time limit on the operator's page; TRAI's regulations were under amendment in 2026.
**Then [FORUM]: Consumer commission** (section 7). **[PARALLEL]:** National Consumer Helpline (section 6) and CPGRAMS (section 8) for systemic issues.

## 4. Airlines

**Rung 1 [PREREQ]: Airline's customer care or grievance channel.** Put the PNR, flight number, date, sector and refund reference in the first lines.
**Rung 2 [PREREQ, recommended]: Airline's Nodal Officer / Appellate Authority**, published on the airline's website under passenger charter or grievance redressal.
**[PARALLEL]: AirSewa** (airsewa.gov.in or the app), run by the Ministry of Civil Aviation. Routes the complaint to the airline with DGCA visibility.
**[FORUM]: Consumer commission** for compensation.
Key entitlements (DGCA Civil Aviation Requirements and Passenger Charter): on airline-caused cancellation, the passenger chooses between a refund and an alternate flight; card refunds are due within about 7 days; vouchers are optional and need the passenger's consent. Confirm current CAR figures before quoting compensation amounts.

## 5. E-commerce (marketplaces, sellers, food delivery, quick commerce, online services)

Under the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020, every platform must appoint and display a **Grievance Officer** who must **acknowledge within 48 hours and redress within one month**.
**Rung 1 [PREREQ]: Platform's customer support**, then the **Grievance Officer** (find the name and email on the platform's grievance page).
**[PARALLEL]: National Consumer Helpline** (section 6). Optional, often effective, not a precondition for the commission.
**[FORUM]: Consumer commission** (section 7).

## 6. National Consumer Helpline (NCH), any sector [PARALLEL]

- Toll-free **1915**, WhatsApp **8800001915**, the NCH app, UMANG, or the **INGRAM** portal at consumerhelpline.gov.in.
- Free, 17 languages. NCH forwards the complaint to the company (many large companies are "convergence partners" and respond through the portal) and gives a docket number.
- A good pre-litigation step: it often resolves matters, and an unresolved NCH docket strengthens a consumer-commission case.

## 7. Consumer Commissions (Consumer Protection Act, 2019) [FORUM, EXCLUSIVE vs ombudsmen]

- File online at **e-jagriti.gov.in** (replaced e-Daakhil from 1 January 2025) or in person.
- **Which commission** is decided by the value of goods or services paid as consideration (Consumer Protection Jurisdiction Rules, 2021):
  - District Commission: up to ₹50 lakh
  - State Commission: above ₹50 lakh up to ₹2 crore
  - National Commission (NCDRC): above ₹2 crore
- **Filing fee:** nil up to ₹5 lakh at the District Commission; small graded fees above that. Confirm the current schedule on e-Jagriti during filing.
- **User deadline: within 2 years** of the cause of action (Section 69). Delay can be condoned for sufficient cause, but don't rely on it.
- The user can file where they live or work, or where the cause of action arose.
- A legal notice to the opposite party before filing is common practice and often triggers settlement.
- Appeals: District to State within 45 days of the order; check the current period for State to National.
- Lawyer optional. Recommend one for larger claims or contested cases.

## 8. Government departments and public services: CPGRAMS [PARALLEL]

- **pgportal.gov.in** or the CPGRAMS app. Covers central ministries and departments, and states that are integrated.
- Resolution due within **21 days**; if more time is needed, the department must send an interim reply.
- If the reply is poor, rate it "Poor" to unlock an **appeal**, which should be decided within 30 days.
- Many states also run their own grievance portals; mention them when the matter is a state subject.
- Check for a **department's own grievance channel** first (Passport Seva, EPFiGMS, income-tax e-Nivaran, Rail Madad and similar). It is often faster, and CPGRAMS can run alongside it.
- **Consumer commission caveat:** commissions have often held that sovereign functions (passports, police, taxation, statutory licensing) are not a "service" under the Consumer Protection Act. Don't recommend a consumer complaint against a government office without flagging this; use CPGRAMS, RTI, the department's appellate officer, or a writ petition through a lawyer.

## 9. RTI (Right to Information Act, 2005): when an office stonewalls [PARALLEL]

Use RTI to get the facts a public authority is sitting on (status of your file, reasons for rejection, action taken on your complaint). It doesn't decide the dispute, but it creates pressure and evidence.
- Online at **rtionline.gov.in** for central authorities; states have their own portals.
- Fee: ₹10 for central authorities (state fees vary; BPL applicants are exempt).
- Reply due within 30 days. First appeal to the First Appellate Authority if the reply is missing or unsatisfactory, then second appeal to the Information Commission.
- Private companies are not covered directly; use RTI on the regulator (RBI, IRDAI, TRAI, DGCA) to ask what action was taken on your complaint.

## 10. Railways: IRCTC e-tickets and Indian Railways services

Two bodies, two channels. **IRCTC** handles e-ticket booking, payment and refund processing and catering contracts. **Indian Railways** (the Zonal Railway) runs the trains and **decides TDR refunds**; IRCTC only forwards them.

**Refund deadlines run in hours, not days.** Missing them kills the refund. Check the time first, before any triage beyond the basics.

**Rung 1 [PREREQ for refund]: File the TDR (Ticket Deposit Receipt)** on irctc.co.in (My Transactions → File TDR) or the IRCTC app. Indicative windows (eRail summary of IRCTC rules effective 12 Nov 2015, last updated 2018; **UNVERIFIED**, confirm on irctc.co.in refund rules before quoting):
- Train late more than 3 hours and you didn't travel: file **before the train's actual departure**.
- Confirmed ticket, didn't travel and couldn't cancel: up to **4 hours** before scheduled departure. RAC: **30 minutes** before.
- AC failure: within **20 hours** of actual arrival.
- Travelled in a lower class / proper coach not attached: within **2 days** of the certificate, post the original certificate to IRCTC.
- Partly travelled, excess fare charged, train terminated short or diverted: about **72 hours**.
- Train **cancelled** by Railways: refund is automatic for e-tickets, no TDR needed.
- Zonal Railways process TDRs slowly (60 days or more is common). Failed booking where money was debited but no ticket issued is normally auto-refunded; if not, also check the RBI failed-transaction TAT (section 1).

**Rung 2 [PREREQ, recommended]: IRCTC eQuery** (equery.irctc.co.in), care@irctc.co.in or etickets@irctc.co.in, e-ticketing helpline **14646**. Quote user ID, PNR, transaction ID, TDR reference.
**[PARALLEL]: Rail Madad** (railmadad.indianrailways.gov.in, helpline **139**) for on-train and station issues (cleanliness, AC, catering, safety, staff conduct) and for stalled refunds. **[PARALLEL]: CPGRAMS** (select Ministry of Railways) and the **National Consumer Helpline** (1915).
**[FORUM]: Consumer commission** for deficiency in service: unprocessed refunds, double charges, catering failures. Commissions have awarded compensation against Railways and IRCTC for delayed refunds.
**[FORUM]: Railway Claims Tribunal** (claims.indianrail.gov.in) for accidents, loss of booked goods and refund of fares under the Railway Claims Tribunal Act, 1987. **Forum choice for pure fare refunds is contested**: courts have differed on whether the tribunal's jurisdiction excludes consumer commissions. For refunds above a few thousand rupees, or anything involving injury or goods, recommend a lawyer before choosing between the tribunal and a commission.

Scam warning: fake "IRCTC customer care" numbers in search ads are common. IRCTC never asks for OTPs, UPI PINs, QR scans or remote-access apps (AnyDesk, TeamViewer).

## Sectors not listed

Electricity (state Electricity Ombudsman via the discom's Consumer Grievance Redressal Forum), real estate (state RERA), securities and brokers (SEBI SCORES, then SMART ODR), pensions and provident fund (EPFiGMS), postal and courier services, and education refunds. Search for the current process before advising, and follow the same pattern: company first, then the sector's internal appellate officer, then the regulator or ombudsman, then the consumer commission.
